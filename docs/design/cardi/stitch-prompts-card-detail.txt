CARDÌ — THE CARD DETAIL SCREEN, FOUR FRAMES
===========================================

The screen you pass through to reach the barcode. It is the only scroll-linked screen in the
app, and the frames exist mainly to pin down that transition.

  A. AT REST     — header IS the brand colour, seamless with the hero. The exemplar.
  B. BLENDING    — hero gone, header fading out over cream, title fading in
  C. CONDENSED   — cream plus a hairline, title settled
  D. CUSTOM CARD — no brand logo; a dark accent, so the controls flip to white

Every prompt is self-contained and repeats its own chrome in full. A prompt may NEVER refer to
another frame. Write every correction as a NUMBERED DEFECT.

Screen source of truth: features/cards/screens/CardDetailScreen.tsx and
features/cards/components/CardDetails.tsx, BrandHero.tsx, DetailRow.tsx; contrast helper in
shared/theme/luminance.ts; strings in shared/i18n/locales/en.ts under `cards.details`.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT READING THE CODE TURNED UP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE HEADER FOLLOWS THE BRAND — AS A TRANSITION, NOT A TINT.

   `headerBg = brand ? brand.color : theme.primary` tints both the navigation header and the
   `BrandHero` band. An earlier draft of this spec took the colour OFF the header on the
   grounds that navigation should not wear a third-party brand's colour. That was wrong: it
   cut a horizontal cream band straight across the top of the hero, so the brand block read as
   interrupted rather than as one field.

   What that argument was actually protecting against is narrower than the rule it produced:
   **brand-coloured chrome with nothing beneath it to identify.** That only happens once the
   hero has scrolled away. So the colour is a transition:

     at rest    the header IS the brand colour, and so is the status-bar inset. The field runs
                unbroken from the top of the screen to the bottom of the hero.
     blending   once the hero has gone, the brand fades out over cream and the title fades in.
     condensed  cream plus a hairline. Nothing is left for the colour to identify.

   The endpoints stay clean — full brand, or full cream — so this never becomes forty-five
   permanent pastel tints. The mid-state is one frame of a transition, not a resting style.

2. THE ORDER OF THE TRANSITION MATTERS. The header stays matched to the hero for as long as
   ANY of the hero is visible: they are one field, and a header a shade paler than the band
   directly beneath it reads as a rendering fault, not a transition. The blend belongs to the
   tail of the scroll, after the band has gone. Drafted the other way round it looked broken.

3. "ONE UNBROKEN FIELD" IS A FACT ABOUT THE MARKUP, NOT ABOUT THE COLOUR VALUES.
   The first HTML draft gave the status-bar inset, the header and the hero each their own
   background, every one set to the same brand colour. It rendered with faint horizontal lines
   at both boundaries — visible enough that ifero read them as navigation-bar borders. Nothing
   drew a border. The element edges land on fractional device pixels (measured at .391 on a 2x
   display), so a hairline of the PARENT's cream leaked between siblings that agreed with each
   other but not with what was behind them. A computed `outline: 1.5px` on every element was
   checked and cleared — `outline-style: none`, so it never paints.

   The fix is structural rather than a tweak: ONE element paints the field and the three sit on
   it transparently. Then there is no second colour in that region left to leak. The seam was
   possible only because "three things that match" is not the same object as "three things on
   one field", and the markup was saying the first.

   **This is a live hazard in the shipped app, not only in the frames.** `headerBg` tints the
   navigation header and `BrandHero` as two separately-filled views; any layout PR that keeps
   them separate inherits the same seam. The band wants one filled parent.

4. THE CONTROLS FLIP WITH LUMINANCE, AND THE HELPER IS ALMOST RIGHT.
   `shared/theme/luminance.ts` returns `#FFFFFF` below 0.5 luminance and `#1F1F24` above — so
   ink on Esselunga's `#FFCC00` (luminance ≈ 0.79) and white on the `#0C843C` accent
   (≈ 0.40). Correct behaviour, but `#1F1F24` is **not** ink `#181824`. Another near-palette
   value; it should be ink.

5. THE CONDENSED HEADER USES A DROP SHADOW. `headerShadowVisible: isHeaderCondensed`. The
   system forbids drop shadows anywhere; a **1px `#D6D6CB` hairline** does the same job and is
   already the established pattern for the anchored footer.

6. THE TITLE IS HIDDEN WITH `fontSize: 0`. At rest the title is `''` at size 0; past the hero
   it becomes the card name at 17. These frames specify the intent instead: **no title at
   rest, card name once condensed** — and in Inter, not Space Grotesk, because it is a
   third-party brand's name doing navigation duty and the display face is ours.

7. THE FAVOURITE STAR IS `theme.warning` — amber. The same off-palette colour already replaced
   on the wallet tile. It becomes **beam `#FCCC0C`** when filled, outline in the foreground
   colour when not. One of the very few places beam legitimately appears: an active state.

8. THERE ARE TWO IMPLEMENTATIONS OF THE BARCODE SCREEN, AND THEY HAVE ALREADY DRIFTED.
   `BarcodeFlash` serves the `barcode/[id]` route; `FullscreenBarcode` opens from inside
   `CardDetails` by tapping the barcode. Both maximise brightness — the critical behaviour is
   intact in both — but `shadowOpacity` is 0.05 vs **0.08** and radius 8 vs **12**, and both
   use Tailwind `#1F2937`. **These should be one component.** Not a design task; a note for the
   tokens and layout PRs.

9. THE BARCODE GETS A WHITE CARD HERE, AND NO PLATE AT ALL ON THE FULLSCREEN VIEW. It looks
   like a contradiction and is not. On cream, and tappable, the card separates the code and
   marks it interactive. On a white field a white plate defines a boundary carrying no
   information. **White card means interactive; bare ground means content.**


Every prompt below is LITERALLY STANDALONE: it repeats the chrome and the content stack in
full and refers to nothing outside its delimiters. Paste one whole block. Write every
correction as a NUMBERED DEFECT.

  > This file previously kept the chrome in a shared "COMMON CHROME" preamble, with each prompt
  > saying "apply the COMMON CHROME above". Frame A came back right and frames B, C and D each
  > invented a DIFFERENT card detail screen — Notes fields, "Code 128" rows, Edit/Share button
  > pairs, a black "Show Barcode" pill, red delete buttons, no barcode at all. A was right only
  > because it sits directly beneath the preamble in this file, so a copy of it tends to take
  > the preamble along. "Above" does not survive a clipboard.
  >
  > The earlier rule — a prompt may never refer to another FRAME — was too narrow. A prompt may
  > not refer to anything outside itself at all. And the tell is diagnostic: a shared missing
  > spec does not produce a shared wrong answer, it produces N independent guesses. Frames that
  > diverge from each other in DIFFERENT directions mean a dangling reference, not a bad
  > instruction.

PROMPT A — AT REST  (the exemplar)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
=========== PASTE EVERYTHING BETWEEN THIS LINE AND "END OF PROMPT A" ===========

Design ONE mobile screen at 393x852, light mode: the card detail screen for Cardì — a
loyalty card wallet — at rest, with the brand hero fully visible.

FOREGROUND COLOUR for every glyph and control sitting on the coloured field:
ink #181824, because Esselunga's yellow is a light fill.

GROUND: the page beneath the header is cream #F0F0E8.

SYSTEM INSETS: 59px top carrying the iOS status bar — "9:41" at the left in Inter 15px
semibold, then signal, wifi and battery icons at the right, all in the foreground colour. 34px
bottom on cream carrying a 140 x 5 fully-rounded pill in ink #181824 at 85% opacity, centred.

HEADER: 56px tall, flat, and NEVER a drop shadow. A back chevron at the left on a 48x48 target,
a favourite star at the right on a 48x48 target.

MARGINS: 20px left and right for everything except the hero band, which is full-bleed to both
frame edges.

THE CONTENT STACK — at 20px margins, in this order, and it is EXACTLY these four things:

  1. a white #FFFFFF card, 16px radius, 1px #D6D6CB outline, NO shadow, holding a centred
     EAN-13 barcode 280 wide x 100 tall in true black #000000 on white with 20px of padding,
     and beneath the bars "Tap to enlarge" in Inter 13px muted #55555F, centred;

  2. a 24px gap, then a white #FFFFFF card, 16px radius, 1px #D6D6CB outline, holding three
     hairline-divided rows — label at the left in Inter 15px muted #55555F, value at the right
     in Inter 15px ink #181824:
         "Number"  /  "2000 0123 4567 8"
         "Color"   /  "Yellow"
         "Added"   /  "12 August 2026"

  3. "MANAGE" in Inter 12px semibold uppercase, letter-spacing 0.05em, muted #55555F;

  4. a white #FFFFFF card, 16px radius, 1px #D6D6CB outline, holding two rows: "Edit card" in
     Inter 17px ink #181824 with a chevron-right at the far right, and "Delete card" in
     Inter 17px #C41E1E with NO chevron.

NOTHING MAY BE ADDED TO THAT STACK. Specifically: no "Notes" field or note card, no "Format" or
"Code 128" or "CODE128" row, no "Card Details" or "CARD DETAILS" section heading, no "Show
Barcode" button, no Edit/Share button pair, no pencil or copy icon, no coloured swatch dot
beside the colour value, and no relabelling of "Number" as "Card Number". The barcode card is
the FIRST item in the stack and is never omitted. "Delete card" is TEXT: no red fill, no red or
pink background, no border, no outline, no trash icon — one line of #C41E1E type in a white
card row.

FORBIDDEN: bottom tab bars, floating action buttons, drop shadows, gradients, glassmorphism,
illustrations, a device bezel or phone frame drawn around the screen, and
coral/salmon/terracotta/orange. Beam #FCCC0C appears ONLY as a filled favourite star.

1. THE TOP INSET AND THE HEADER ARE BOTH FILLED #FFCC00, Esselunga's own colour, and so is
   the hero beneath them. Paint it as ONE filled region containing all three, not three regions
   that happen to share a value: three separately filled boxes leave visible hairlines where
   they meet. There must be NO seam, NO divider and NO change of tone from the very top of the
   screen down to the bottom of the hero band — one unbroken field of #FFCC00.

2. NO TITLE in the header at rest. Back chevron at the left, star OUTLINE at the right, and
   nothing at all between them.

3. THE HERO: full-bleed edge to edge, 200px tall, #FFCC00, with the Esselunga wordmark centred
   at about 32px in ink #181824 — ink, not white, because the fill is light. No gradient, no
   overlay, no shadow, no rounded corners, no inset.

4. Below the hero the page is cream #F0F0E8. The card name "Esselunga" in Space Grotesk 24px
   BOLD ink #181824 — a heading, not a caption: not 13px, not grey, not muted. 16px of space
   above and below it. Then the content stack, complete, ending with the Edit/Delete card.

TONE
The brand owns the top of the screen as one continuous block and stops dead at the bottom
of the hero. Everything below is cream, white cards and ink — the same furniture as settings.
