CARDÌ — THE CAPTURE PATTERN, FIVE FRAMES
========================================

How a card gets into the wallet. Two screens: you choose the brand, then you point the camera
at its barcode. This is the only pattern in the app that is a CAMERA rather than a layout, and
it is the only one where the design system contradicts itself — productively.

  A. CHOOSE     — the searchable brand list. The way in.
  B. AIM        — the viewfinder, camera live, brand known. The exemplar.
  C. NO CAMERA  — permission refused. The one dark screen that goes cream.
  D. MANY CODES — an imported image held more than one barcode.
  E. NO CODE    — an imported image held none.

Every prompt below is LITERALLY STANDALONE: it repeats the chrome and the content in full and
refers to nothing outside its delimiters. Paste one whole block. Write every correction as a
NUMBERED DEFECT.

  > The rule the hard way: a prompt may not refer to another frame OR to a shared preamble.
  > "Apply the COMMON CHROME above" does not survive a clipboard — it cost three card-detail
  > frames, which each invented a different screen. Divergence in DIFFERENT directions is the
  > signature of a dangling reference; divergence in the SAME direction is a bad instruction.

Screen source of truth: app/add-card/index.tsx → features/add-card/screens/
CardTypeSelectionScreen.tsx (with BrandSearchBar, BrandList, BrandRow, BrandPill, OtherCardRow,
useBrandSearch); app/add-card/scan.tsx → BrandScannerScreen.tsx (with ScannerOverlay,
FloatingBackButton, NoCodeFoundBanner, MultiCodePickerSheet, useImageScan, and the shared
useBarcodeScanner from features/cards/hooks). Strings in shared/i18n/locales/en.ts under
`addCard`.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT READING THE CODE TURNED UP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THERE IS ONE VIEWFINDER, NOT TWO. The plan carried `scan` and `add-card/scan` as "two
   viewfinders to unify". `app/scan.tsx` is a bare `<Redirect>` to `/add-card/scan` — a legacy
   bridge from the old entrypoint, still reached from `CatalogueGrid`. There is nothing to
   unify. Half of this work item did not exist.

2. THE SCAN IS BRAND-FIRST, SO THERE IS NO "BRAND NOT FOUND". This was expected to be the
   interesting fork — scan a code, look it up, and design the moment it is not among the 45
   catalogue brands. It cannot happen. You pick the brand from a searchable list and *then*
   scan; `BrandScannerScreen` receives `brandId` and shows a `BrandPill`, and the catalogue is
   consulted before the camera opens, never after. The custom-card path is a **list row**
   ("Other card"), which routes into the very same scanner with `mode: 'custom'`.

   So the door into a custom card is already built. It is just not where anyone looked for it.

3. THE REAL JUNCTION IS NO CODE / ONE CODE / MANY CODES, PLUS NO CAMERA. Five states live on
   the scanner screen, not two: camera ready; permission denied; camera mount error; an
   imported image being decoded; that image failing (`NoCodeFoundBanner`, with **three**
   distinct reasons — `notFound`, `scanFailed`, `pickerFailed`); and that image yielding
   several codes (`MultiCodePickerSheet`, up to six rows).

4. THE DESIGN SYSTEM CONTRADICTS ITSELF HERE, AND IT RESOLVES CLEANLY.
   `stitch-prompts-barcode.txt` forbids, by name: "a frame, a corner marker, a viewfinder
   bracket". `ScannerOverlay` draws white corner brackets AND an animated scan line over a live
   camera. Both are correct.

   That rule governs the barcode you are **displaying** — your own card, held up to a scanner,
   where anything on top of the bars costs you a read. This screen is the barcode you are
   **reading** — someone else's code, through a lens, where the overlay IS the affordance that
   tells the person where to aim. **Same mark, opposite verdict, decided by which way the light
   is travelling.** Nothing needs weakening; the rule needed a direction.

5. AND THIS IS THE ONE SCREEN WHERE BEAM IS MANDATORY RATHER THAN BANNED. The scan line is
   `theme.primary`, which the design asset resolves to `#000000` — a 2px near-black line, over a
   camera feed, under a 40% black scrim. Effectively invisible, and nobody would notice because
   the screen still works.

   Meanwhile the brand's entire ornament is, in the design system's own words, "a **scan beam**
   passing over a barcode", and the barcode-display spec banned beam from that screen with the
   line "the real beam comes from the scanner at the till". **On this screen we ARE the
   scanner.** The beam is ours to draw, at `#FCCC0C`, exactly here and nowhere else. The
   identity's central metaphor has precisely one screen where it is literal.

6. THE VIEWFINDER IS A SQUARE AND WHAT IT FRAMES IS NOT. `viewfinderSize = screenWidth * 0.7`
   is used as both width and height — a ~275px square. An EAN-13 is roughly 95 : 20. A QR is
   1 : 1. And the screen **already knows which to expect**: `expectedFormat={brand?.defaultFormat}`
   is threaded from the catalogue into the overlay and on into the scanner hook.

   So the brackets can match the code — wide and short for a linear format, square for QR. A
   square bracket around a linear barcode teaches the wrong gesture: people centre the code in
   the box and end up standing too far back, which is the one failure mode a viewfinder exists
   to prevent.

7. THE FAILURE STATES LEAVE THE DARK, AND THAT IS RIGHT. Permission-denied and camera-error
   render on `theme.background` — cream — with ordinary cream-page furniture, while the live
   screen is `#000000`. It looks like an inconsistency and is not. It is the same logic as the
   card-detail header: **the dark ground exists to serve the camera. No camera, no dark.**
   Ratified, not changed.

8. CREAM, NOT WHITE, FOR TEXT ON THE DARK. Every glyph over the camera is `#FFFFFF`. The design
   system's dark mode specifies body text as cream `#F0F0E8`. Over a live feed under a scrim,
   cream reads warmer and matches the rest of the app. The corner brackets stay pure white —
   they are a mark, not text.

9. THE TEXT SHADOW STAYS. `textShadowColor: 'rgba(0,0,0,0.75)'` on the instruction line. The
   system forbids shadows everywhere. This one is legibility over a background nobody controls,
   not decoration — the same class of exception as the barcode's quiet zone, where the space is
   an input to the scanner rather than a margin. **Ratified explicitly so it stops reading as
   drift.** It applies only to type set directly on the camera feed.

10. `theme.warning` AGAIN — THIRD SIGHTING. `NoCodeFoundBanner` puts `warning-amber` at
    `theme.warning` beside its message. The same off-palette amber already replaced on the
    wallet tile and the favourite star. Here the honest fix is to **delete the icon**: the
    banner is already a dark plate carrying a sentence, and a 20px amber triangle on it is
    decoration doing no work.

11. THE BANNER'S ACTION LINKS ARE INVISIBLE FOR THE SAME REASON AS THE SCAN LINE. Both links
    are `theme.primary` on a `rgba(0,0,0,0.80)` plate — near-black on near-black. They become
    beam `#FCCC0C`, which on this screen is already established as the interaction colour.

12. 48px MaterialIcons STANDING IN FOR ILLUSTRATIONS. `no-photography` and `error-outline` at
    48px on the two failure screens. The system has no icon at that size — icons are 24px on a
    48px target — so a 48px glyph is an illustration placeholder, the same note the wallet
    `EmptyState` was pulled up for. Left as a plain icon here; illustrations are Wave B.

13. MARGINS DRIFT, THIRD SIGHTING. `bottomActions` and `centeredContent` are both
    `paddingHorizontal: 24`. Joins `CardForm` 32 and settings 24. The resolved rule is 16 for
    the derived grid, 20 for everything else.

14. THE BRAND PILL IS POSITIONED OFF THE BACK BUTTON, NOT THE GRID.
    `insets.top + SPACING.sm + TOUCH_TARGET.min + SPACING.md` — four values added at the call
    site. It lands at a sane place today and will drift the moment any one of them moves.

COLOURS ARE LITERAL HEXES, NEVER TOKENS. Write #181824, #FCCC0C, #F0F0E8, #FFFFFF,
#D6D6CB, #55555F and #C41E1E exactly as written. Do NOT reach for a semantic colour name
instead — not `primary`, not `on-surface`, not `on-background`, not `surface`, not `outline`,
not `outline-variant`, and not a Tailwind utility built on one such as text-primary,
bg-primary, border-primary or border-outline-variant. In this project's theme `primary`
resolves to #000000 and `on-surface` to #1a1c18, so a screen assembled out of tokens comes
out in two wrong near-blacks that look almost right and are not. A literal hex survives; a
token does not.
