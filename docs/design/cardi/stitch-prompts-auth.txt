CARDÌ — THE ACCOUNT PATTERN, SIX FRAMES
=======================================

Everything to do with having an account. It looks like six screens and it is ONE SHAPE with five
fillings — the code already made that reduction, and nobody had noticed.

  A. SIGN IN         — the exemplar. Two fields, one link, one button, one way out.
  B. CREATE ACCOUNT  — the longest form: strength meter and a consent checkbox.
  C. FORGOT PASSWORD — the shortest. One field.
  D. CODE            — the 8-digit code, with a resend cooldown. Serves TWO routes.
  E. NEW PASSWORD    — two fields and the strength meter.
  F. REQUEST FAILED  — the error idiom, which is the decision this pattern turns on.

Every prompt below is LITERALLY STANDALONE: it repeats the chrome and the content in full and
refers to nothing outside its delimiters. Paste one whole block. Write every correction as a
NUMBERED DEFECT.

Screen source of truth: features/auth/SignInScreen.tsx, CreateAccountScreen.tsx,
ForgotPasswordScreen.tsx, VerifyEmailScreen.tsx, NewPasswordScreen.tsx and the 13-line
RecoveryOtpScreen.tsx; shared components in features/auth/components/ — AuthScreenLayout,
AppIconHeader, AuthLink, ErrorBanner, PasswordInput, PasswordStrengthIndicator. Strings in
shared/i18n/locales/en.ts under `auth`.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT READING THE CODE TURNED UP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. AUTH IS ALREADY ONE PATTERN, AND THE CODE GOT THERE FIRST.
   `AuthScreenLayout` is a single shape: a keyboard-aware scroll view,
   `paddingHorizontal: layout.screenHorizontalMargin`, an optional app-icon circle, an optional
   centred `title1` heading, an optional `subheadline` subtitle, then `children` — vertically
   centred by default. All five screens are that layout plus a different `children` block,
   sharing `AuthLink`, `ErrorBanner`, `PasswordInput` and `PasswordStrengthIndicator`.

   It is also **already on the correct 24px margin**, because it reads the token rather than a
   literal. Nothing in this pattern needs the margin fix that the rest of the app needs.

2. AND IT IS FIVE SCREENS, NOT SIX. `RecoveryOtpScreen.tsx` is thirteen lines:
   `const RecoveryOtpScreen = () => <VerifyEmailScreen purpose="recovery" />`. The verify-email
   and password-recovery screens are one component with a prop — which is why
   `VerifyEmailScreen` is 552 lines while nothing else in the folder passes 300.

3. THERE IS A SECOND STOCK-ICON PLACEHOLDER, AND IT IS A DIFFERENT ONE.
   `AppIconHeader` renders `MaterialCommunityIcons name="card-account-details-outline"` at 36px
   inside an 80px circle filled `theme.primary` at 10%. Onboarding's `BrandedIcon` renders
   `MaterialIcons name="credit-card"` at 34px inside a 100px circle.

   So the app carries **two different generic glyphs standing in for a logo it does not have**,
   in two different flows, at two different sizes. The onboarding spec called its version the
   highest-leverage fix in that flow; this is the same fix, and finding the second one is the
   argument for drawing the ì once as a real asset rather than patching each site.

4. THE ERROR IDIOM CONTRADICTS THE FORM PATTERN, AND BOTH SIDES ARE RIGHT.
   `stitch-prompts-form-states.txt` is explicit, and it was accepted: a field error is a 1px
   `#C41E1E` outline plus 13px helper text below, and — quoting it — "do not add an error icon
   inside the field, and do not tint the field's fill", "do not add a summary banner at the top
   of the screen".

   `ErrorBanner` is exactly what that forbids: a summary banner at the top of the form, with a
   fill tinted `theme.error` at 8% and an `error-outline` icon.

   The rule needed a direction, the same way the beam rule and the quiet zone did:

     a VALIDATION error   belongs to a FIELD. "Email is required." The field is what is wrong,
                          so the field carries it — 1px #C41E1E outline, 13px helper beneath,
                          no icon, no fill tint, and never more than one field at a time.
     a REQUEST error      belongs to the SUBMISSION. "Unable to connect." "Incorrect email or
                          password." There is no field to attach a dropped connection to, so
                          it needs one banner above the form. The form spec's prohibition was
                          written about validation and was never tested against a server reply.

   **The banner stays, and it loses the tint and the icon.** On cream, the system's plate is a
   white card with a hairline — so the request-error banner is a white #FFFFFF card with a 1px
   `#C41E1E` outline and the message in `#C41E1E`. That honours "do not tint the fill" while
   allowing the banner the form spec forbade, and it keeps `#C41E1E` to two uses: the outline
   and the sentence.

5. A NETWORK FAILURE CURRENTLY REDDENS BOTH FIELDS. `SignInScreen` computes
   `hasFormError = Boolean(error)` and passes `hasError={hasFormError}` to **both** the email
   field and the password field. So "Unable to connect. Check your internet and try again."
   marks your email address and your password as invalid, though neither is.

   That contradicts the form spec's "exactly one field is in error", and it is wrong on its own
   terms: a dropped connection is not a claim about your typing. In these frames a request error
   colours the banner and nothing else.

6. BODY COPY BELOW THE MINIMUM, AGAIN, IN THE TWO MOST-READ COMPONENTS. `AuthLink` sets the
   whole line — "Don't have an account? Create one" — at `typography.footnote`, and `ErrorBanner`
   sets its message at `typography.footnote`. The system's minimum body size is 15px and its
   smallest legitimate size is `label-bold` 13px, which is a LABEL. An error message is the copy
   a person reads most carefully in the whole app, and it is set smaller than everything else.

7. `theme.link` — THIRD FLOW, STILL NOT IN THE PALETTE. Onboarding uses it, auth uses it for
   "Forgot password?", for the action half of every `AuthLink`, and for the recovery links. The
   palette defines ink, beam, cream and deep blue; deep blue is "secondary structure only… never
   the primary action colour". These frames use **ink #181824** for links, underlined only inside
   prose.

8. THE TWO CODE SCREENS DUPLICATE TWELVE STRINGS. `auth.verifyEmail` and `auth.recoveryOtp` are
   byte-identical apart from `heading` and `subtitle` — confirm, resendCode, resendIn,
   wrongEmail, goBack, incorrectCode, expiredCode, verifyUnavailable, resendFailure,
   resendSuccess. One shared component reads from two copied string sets, so every copy fix has
   to be made twice and there is no test that they agree.

9. THE PASSWORD RULE IS STATED THREE TIMES, IN THREE WORDINGS.
   `placeholders.newPassword` = "Min 8 chars, 1 letter, 1 number";
   `validation.passwordRule` = "Min 8 characters, at least one letter and one number.";
   `createAccount.passwordRequirements` = "Password must be at least 8 characters with at least
   one letter and one number." Same rule, three registers. The frames use the placeholder for
   the field and the validation string for the error, and drop the third.

10. THE STRENGTH METER IS THE ONE PLACE A THIRD COLOUR IS UNAVOIDABLE.
    `getPasswordStrength` returns weak / fair / strong, and a meter needs three distinguishable
    states. The palette has no "good" colour: beam is the signature and `#C41E1E` is failure.
    These frames use **ink at three weights of a bar** — one, two or three filled segments in
    ink #181824 against #D6D6CB — so strength is carried by COUNT rather than by hue.

    > **The accessibility half of this argument was overstated and is withdrawn.** The shipped
    > meter is a single bar whose WIDTH goes 33% / 66% / 100% as well as changing hue, so it
    > already carries a non-colour channel and a colour-blind reader is not stuck. The real
    > objection is narrower and holds on its own: the shipped meter reaches for
    > `theme.error` / `theme.warning` / `theme.success`, and **`success` is a green that does not
    > exist in the Cardì palette at all.** Counting in ink needs no new colour.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
COMMON SHAPE — for humans reading this file, not for pasting
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every frame: cream #F0F0E8 ground, 59px status bar in ink, 34px home indicator, 24px margins, no
header bar and no title bar — the app icon and the heading do that job. Content is centred
vertically. The stack is always: icon, heading, subtitle, [banner], fields, [link], button,
footer link. A 52px ink button with white 17px semibold type. Fields are 48px minimum, white,
12px radius, 1px #D6D6CB outline, label always visible above.

Signing in is a chore standing between someone and their cards, so the screen is two fields and
