Cardì · palette bench · 11 August 2026

Five directions. The same five colours.

Nothing below invents a new hue. Every specimen uses the identical palette pulled from the Cardì logo bars. What changes between them is where colour is allowed to live — as a single accent, as a whole surface, as the card itself, as a wash, or as an edge glowing in the dark. That is the real decision, and it is the one that determines whether the app reads as playful or as chaotic.

The barcode carries no beam. The beam belongs to the scanner at the till — it is the shop's, not ours. Drawing our own across a scan target would be decoration impersonating function, and it would cost contrast on the one screen that cannot afford any. The ì keeps the beam as a promise; the barcode screen keeps it by getting out of the way. What is left to judge on those frames is the surround, which turns out to separate these five more sharply than the wallet does.

SPECIMEN 01 · COLOUR AS ACCENT

Ink & Beam

Good stationery. The beam is the only ornament, and it is spent twice.

Cream ground, white cards, ink actions. The five hues appear only as virtual-logo tiles. Yellow is reserved for the ì, focus rings, and the scan beam — then takes over as the action colour in the dark, which is the one moment of theatre.

What it risks Sober to the point of severe. All the playfulness rests on a 3-pixel dot, and if that dot isn't animated in the real build, there is no play at all.

SPECIMEN 02 · COLOUR AS SURFACE

Beam Forward

The app is yellow the way a taxi is yellow. You spot it across a room.

Yellow stops being an accent and becomes architecture — a full beam-coloured header block with ink text sitting on it. Cards stay white on cream below. Ownable instantly; nobody confuses this with another wallet app.

What it risks Hazard tape. Yellow at surface scale reads “caution” before it reads “brand”, and once yellow is everywhere the beam stops being special. It also loses the barcode screen: a saturated surround at full brightness is glare beside the scan target.

SPECIMEN 03 · COLOUR AS THE CARD

Five Bars Alive

The logo, scrolling. Every card is one of the bars, and the wallet is the mark itself.

The five hues come off the bench and become the card surfaces. Chrome stays ink on cream; the list is the colour. Conceptually the tightest idea here — brand and content become literally the same object.

What it risks This is the chaos you named. Real brand logos will sit on these fills and fight them, the yellow card needs ink text while the others need white, and with fourteen cards the palette repeats and stops meaning anything. Its barcode screen inherits the card's hue for the surround, which is the worst of the five for the scan.

SPECIMEN 04 · COLOUR AS WASH

Gelato

Colour you can read straight through. Playful in the surface, calm in the text.

The same five hues, but the card takes a 10% wash of its accent while the tile stays fully saturated. Text stays ink on every row, so contrast never moves and the list never shouts. Colour becomes a wayfinding cue rather than a costume.

What it risks Washes drift toward nursery pastel, and at 10% the blue and the azure are nearly the same card — the cue you added may not survive a glance in a dim shop.

SPECIMEN 05 · COLOUR AS EDGE

Night Market

Built for the checkout at nine in the evening. Dark is the front door, not the alternative.

Black ground, ink cards, and each card carries a 3px accent edge that glows against it. Beam yellow drives every action. Light mode still exists and is properly designed — it simply is not the face the product leads with.

What it risks This is a product decision disguised as a palette. Ship it and the light theme becomes the neglected one — the exact failure the last design system had, just pointing the other way.

How to choose

Don't pick the one that looks best on this page — a comparison board flatters bold options. Pick against the three questions that decide whether it survives contact with the product:

  1. Can you find one card among fourteen in a single glance? Scroll each light frame and look only at row six, where the palette starts repeating.
  2. Does a real brand logo survive on that card surface? Every specimen here shows initials tiles. Half your cards will carry actual logos with their own colours.
  3. Is the beam still special? If yellow appears more than twice per screen, the signature has become decoration.
  4. Does the barcode surround stay out of the way? Nothing may overlay the bars, and the field around them should be neutral — black, cream or white. 02 and 03 both fail here, and it is the screen that matters most.

They are not mutually exclusive, either. 04 Gelato with 01's chrome is a real hybrid — washed cards, ink actions, beam held back for the scan. So is 03 with a user-chosen colour per card, which converts Carson's repeat problem into a feature.

The specimens render in their own fixed colours regardless of your system theme — only the board around them follows it. Type is the system sans rather than Space Grotesk and Inter, since webfonts can't load here; judge colour and structure, not letterforms.
