CARDÌ — THE PITCH PATTERN, SIX FRAMES
=====================================

The first ninety seconds. Three screens before the person owns a single card, and the only flow
in the app that contains no user content at all — which is exactly why it is the one place our
own voice has to carry the screen, and the one place it currently doesn't.

  A. WELCOME     — the mark, the promise, the way in. The exemplar.
  B. MODES       — on this device, or synced. The only real question we ask.
  C. DIFFERENCE  — the sheet behind "What's the difference?"
  D. HIGHLIGHT 1 — all your cards in one place.
  E. HIGHLIGHT 2 — scan or add manually. The beam motif, used literally.
  F. HIGHLIGHT 3 — your data, your rules. The button changes and the dots move.

Every prompt below is LITERALLY STANDALONE: it repeats the chrome and the content in full and
refers to nothing outside its delimiters. Paste one whole block. Write every correction as a
NUMBERED DEFECT.

  > The rule the hard way: a prompt may not refer to another frame OR to a shared preamble.
  > "Apply the COMMON CHROME above" does not survive a clipboard — it cost three card-detail
  > frames, which each invented a different screen.

Screen source of truth: app/welcome.tsx → features/onboarding/screens/WelcomeScreen.tsx;
app/onboarding/mode-selection.tsx → ModeSelectionScreen.tsx (with ModeOptionCard,
InfoTooltipModal, useModeSelection); app/onboarding/highlights.tsx →
FeatureHighlightsScreen.tsx (with HighlightSlide, PaginationDots, useHighlightPagination);
shared components BrandedIcon and FannedCardIllustration. Strings in
shared/i18n/locales/en.ts under `onboarding`.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT READING THE CODE TURNED UP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE FIRST THING ANYONE SEES IS CLIP ART. `BrandedIcon` defaults to
   `MaterialIcons name="credit-card"`, tinted `theme.primary`, inside a 100px circle filled
   `theme.primary` at 10% alpha. The welcome screen renders it at 100px with a 34px glyph.

   The brand's entire ornament is the **ì** — *"at rest it is a dot, in motion it is a scan beam
   passing over a barcode"* — and the screen that introduces the brand shows a stock credit-card
   icon instead. This is the single highest-leverage fix in the flow and it is not a design
   question; it is a missing asset.

2. WAVE B WAS NEVER BLOCKED ON INVENTING ILLUSTRATIONS. It was blocked on a belief. There are
   **four** illustrations already in onboarding, all placeholders, all built the same way — as
   monochrome alpha-tints of `theme.primary` assembled out of plain `View`s:

     welcome        `FannedCardIllustration` — three 80x50 rounded rects at 15% / 45% / 75%
                    alpha, offset 30px and 8px. No rotation, so it is not a fan; it is a
                    staircase, and the component name overstates it.
     highlight 1    a 160px circle at 10% alpha holding four 40x34 rects at 50% and 65%.
     highlight 2    a 160px circle holding a 72x50 rect and seven bars of alternating 3px / 2px
                    width — a barcode diagram, and the closest of the four to being right.
     highlight 3    a 160px circle holding `MaterialIcons name="verified-user"` at 60px.

   **The slots already exist at known sizes** — one 200 x 80 band and three 160px circles. The
   job was never "design an illustration system", it was "draw four things into four holes".

3. AND SLIDE 3'S ILLUSTRATION CONTRADICTS ITS OWN COPY. A `verified-user` shield promises
   **security**. The text under it reads "Export and import your cards anytime. No lock-in, no
   hidden fees. Your cards belong to you." — which promises **portability**. Those are different
   claims, and the picture is making the weaker one.

4. `fontSize: 42 - 16`. Left in the source of `ModeSelectionScreen` as arithmetic. It evaluates
   to 26px, which is not a size on the type scale at all (the scale runs 34 / 24 / 20 / 17 / 15 /
   13). It renders fine, which is why it survived.

5. BODY TEXT BELOW THE SYSTEM MINIMUM, IN SEVEN PLACES. The design system says "minimum body size
   is 15px". Onboarding ships: the mode-selection subtitle at `typography.footnote`, its footer at
   13, "What's the difference?" at 14, the `ModeOptionCard` subtitle at 14, its eyebrow at 12, the
   "Recommended" badge at 12, and the highlights "Skip" at 14. The smallest legitimate size in
   this system is `label-bold` 13/600/0.02em, and it is a LABEL, not body copy.

6. THE GRID IS IGNORED ALMOST COMPLETELY. Against "strict 8px grid; 4px only for
   micro-adjustment", the vertical rhythm reads: welcome 64 / 38 / 6 / 44 / 74 / 18; mode
   selection 30 / 8 / 28 / 34 / 4; highlights 8 / 4 / 20 / 10. Of those fourteen values, four are
   multiples of eight. This is the flow that sets a first impression of how carefully the app is
   built.

7. THREE CONSECUTIVE SCREENS, THREE DIFFERENT TOP TREATMENTS. Welcome has no header and
   `paddingTop: insets.top + 64`. Mode selection has a 56px header WITH a 1px bottom border and
   `paddingHorizontal: 12`. Highlights has no header but a right-aligned "Skip" at
   `insets.top + 8`. Nothing in the flow agrees with anything else about where the top of a
   screen is.

8. `theme.link` IS NOT IN THE DESIGN SYSTEM. All three screens use it for text links. The palette
   defines ink, beam, cream and deep blue, and deep blue is reserved for "secondary structure
   only… never the primary action colour". A link needs a defined value; these frames use **ink
   #181824**, underlined only where the link sits inside prose.

9. `theme.primary` IS CARRYING NINE JOBS. The back chevron, the mode-card icon, that icon's
   circle tint, the eyebrow text, the "Recommended" badge fill, the recommended card's border and
   background wash, the active pagination dot, and all four illustrations. In this system
   `primary` is ink, so most of those become grey washes of ink — which is nearly right, and it
   means the illustrations currently have no accent at all.

10. THE RECOMMENDED CARD USES A 2px BORDER AND A 5% WASH. `borderWidth: recommended ? 2 : 1` plus
    `backgroundColor` at `theme.primary` 5%. The system says hairline outlines at 1px and cards
    are white on cream. A selected state does need to be visible, so these frames keep a
    distinction but express it as **ink 1px hairline vs ink 2px hairline plus a beam eyebrow** —
    no background wash, so the card stays white like every other card in the app.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DECISION THIS PATTERN TURNS ON
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ONBOARDING IS THE ONLY FLOW WITH NO USER CONTENT IN IT, AND THAT CHANGES WHAT THE PALETTE IS FOR.

Everywhere else in this app the rule is "the content is the colour": forty-five brand colours are
the content, so our chrome stays quiet and beam is rationed to almost nothing. On these three
screens there are no cards yet. Nothing is competing. The quiet exists to protect something that
is not there.

So this is where the brand speaks in its own voice — and the design system already licenses
exactly that, in a line nobody has applied: **"Illustrations: flat, two-tone (ink line-work on
cream) with beam yellow as the single accent."**

  > **CORRECTED after drawing it.** The first version of this table replaced the four TINTED
  > rectangles with four OUTLINED rectangles, and ifero was right to call the result terrible.
  > Outlining a box is an improvement in colour discipline and nothing else — the placeholders
  > were boxes and the replacements were also boxes. "Flat two-tone ink line-work" does not mean
  > "rectangles with a 2px stroke"; line-work implies drawing.
  >
  > Worse, the welcome fan reproduced the exact defect finding 2 criticises in the shipped one:
  > offsets with no rotation, which makes a staircase, not a fan.

Applied concretely — at most one beam element per drawing:

  the mark         the **ì** — an ink circle, a white stem, and the grave accent as a short
                   beam-yellow STROKE, not a dot. At rest a dot, in motion a beam, so the accent
                   is the beam. Not a credit card, not a wallet, not a generic card glyph.

  welcome band     three card outlines in 2px ink line-work, ROTATED about a shared pivot below
                   the group the way cards held in a hand are — about -17°, -1° and +15°. The
                   frontmost carries a real barcode in ink and one short beam bar beneath it.
                   Rotation is what makes it a fan; offsets alone make a staircase.

  highlight 1      NOT line-work. A MINIATURE OF THE REAL WALLET: four rounded tiles in four
                   true brand colours — Esselunga #FFCC00, Conad #E2231A, Coop #0C84CC, Lidl
                   #0050AA — two by two, filled, no outlines and no beam.

                   This is not an exception to "the content is the colour", it is a
                   demonstration of it. The slide's promise literally is "your brands,
                   together", and the product's whole visual identity is many clashing brand
                   hues. A symbol for "many cards" would be weaker than a small true picture of
                   the thing — and four outlined rectangles two-by-two reads as a keypad.

  highlight 2      one card outline in ink holding a REAL EAN-13 — varied bar widths, because it
                   encodes an actual number — and a single beam-yellow horizontal line sweeping
                   across it, running past both edges of the card. The scan beam, the brand's
                   own motif, used literally. Same verdict as the scanner screen: when WE are
                   the one reading the code, the beam is ours to draw.

  highlight 3      a DASHED ink boundary, and a card crossing out of it at a slight tilt,
                   carrying a real barcode and one beam band. "No lock-in" has a shape: the
                   boundary around your data is permeable and the card leaves freely.

                   NOT a shield, a padlock or a tick — the copy promises portability, not
                   security. And not a pair of arrows either: arrows read as a UML sketch rather
                   than a drawing.

That is five drawings, at two sizes, with at most one accent each. A morning's work, not a
blocked epic.

PROMPT A — WELCOME  (the exemplar)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
=========== PASTE EVERYTHING BETWEEN THIS LINE AND "END OF PROMPT A" ===========

Design ONE mobile screen at 393x852, light mode: the welcome screen of Cardì, a loyalty card
wallet. It is the very first screen a new person sees, before they own any cards.

GROUND: cream #F0F0E8 for the whole frame, both system insets included.

SYSTEM INSETS: 59px top carrying the iOS status bar — "9:41" at the left in Inter 15px
semibold, then signal, wifi and battery icons at the right, all in ink #181824. 34px bottom on
cream carrying a 140 x 5 fully-rounded pill in ink #181824 at 85% opacity, centred.

NO HEADER. There is no navigation bar, no title bar, no back control and nothing to go back to.

MARGINS: 24px left and right.

1. THE MARK, centred, 64px below the status bar: a 96px fully-round circle filled ink #181824,
   holding the letter "ì" in white #FFFFFF, Space Grotesk bold at about 44px — a lowercase i
   with a grave accent. The accent stroke above the i is BEAM #FCCC0C rather than white, and it
   is the only yellow on this screen. This is the brand's logo: do NOT draw a credit card, a
   wallet, a card stack, a barcode, a shopping bag or any generic icon in this circle.

2. THE TITLE "Cardì" centred 32px beneath the circle, in Space Grotesk bold 34px, ink #181824 —
   ALL of it, the grave accent on the final ì included.

   (An earlier draft asked for that accent in beam. Drawing it disproved the idea: beam on cream
   fails contrast, which is exactly why the design system pairs beam with ink and never with
   cream. The beam accent lives on the mark in point 1, where it sits on an ink circle and has
   the contrast to carry it. One accent, on the surface that can hold it.)

3. THE SUBTITLE "Your loyalty cards, always with you" centred 8px beneath the title, in Inter
   17px muted #55555F, on one line.

4. THE ILLUSTRATION, centred 48px beneath the subtitle, occupying about 230 x 110: three
   loyalty-card outlines drawn as 2px INK #181824 line-work on the cream — outlines only, no
   fills beyond the cream ground, no gradients, no shadows, each about 80 x 52 with a 7px
   radius.

   THEY MUST BE ROTATED, not merely offset: pivot all three about a shared point BELOW the
   group, at roughly -17 degrees, -1 degree and +15 degrees, the way three cards held in one
   hand splay apart. Offsetting them diagonally without rotation produces a staircase, not a
   fan, and that is the single most common way this drawing goes wrong.

   The frontmost card carries a real barcode in ink — vertical bars of VARYING widths, not
   evenly spaced stripes — and one short horizontal BEAM #FCCC0C bar beneath the bars. That bar
   is the only yellow in the drawing.

5. THE PRIMARY BUTTON, full width between the margins, sitting 72px beneath the illustration:
   52px tall, filled ink #181824, 12px radius, with "Get Started" centred in Inter 17px semibold
   white #FFFFFF.

6. BENEATH IT, 16px lower, "I already have an account" centred on a 48px tall target in Inter
   15px semibold ink #181824. Plain text — no fill, no outline, no underline, no chevron.

Nothing else on the screen. No pagination dots, no skip link, no progress bar, no card grid, no
photograph, no decorative pattern.

COLOURS ARE LITERAL HEXES, NEVER TOKENS. Write #181824, #FCCC0C, #F0F0E8, #FFFFFF,
#D6D6CB, #55555F and #C41E1E exactly as written. Do NOT reach for a semantic colour name
instead — not `primary`, not `on-surface`, not `on-background`, not `surface`, not `outline`,
not `outline-variant`, and not a Tailwind utility built on one such as text-primary,
bg-primary, border-primary or border-outline-variant. In this project's theme `primary`
resolves to #000000 and `on-surface` to #1a1c18, so a screen assembled out of tokens comes
out in two wrong near-blacks that look almost right and are not. A literal hex survives; a
token does not.

FORBIDDEN: bottom tab bars, floating action buttons, drop shadows, gradients, glassmorphism, a
device bezel or phone frame around the screen, coral/salmon/terracotta/orange, stock icons of
credit cards or wallets, and any second use of beam #FCCC0C beyond the accent on the ì inside
the circle and the one bar in the illustration.

TONE
There are no cards on this screen and nothing competing for attention, which is exactly why it
is the one place the brand may speak in its own voice. Everything is centred, generously spaced
and calm. The ì is the whole identity: a dot at rest, a scan beam in motion.
