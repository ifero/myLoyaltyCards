Card — the complication, all four families

Story 23.1, AC12. WatchComplicationWidget.swift:89-94 declares all four accessory families, so all four are designed. (ComplicationProvider.swift declares none — it is App-Group state persistence, not a widget.) The complication has one job: get you into the wallet in one tap. It is not a data readout.

The four families

The 76 px budget is a gate, not a guideline

ComplicationImage.swift downsamples to maxPoint 38 × scale 2 = 76 px. WidgetKit measures a raster image's native pixel size and rejects anything over the per-family budget with imageTooLarge, which renders the slot as a grey placeholder. .frame and .scaledToFit change layout, not the archived bitmap — they do not help.

Do not derive a looser budget from the source comment. It states the accessoryCorner ceiling two ways — ≈ 46 pt on one line and ≈ 81.6 px three lines below — and those do not reconcile at ×2. 76 px is the shipped, working value; design to it. Nothing on this sheet depends on which reading is right, because 76 is under both.

In situ

The tinted check — every family must read as SHAPE

Watch faces render complications in a tinted mode where the system recolours the content, so no design may depend on its own hue. Below is the same set with colour removed. The ì mark survives because it is a shape; a design that separated beam from ink by colour alone would collapse into one flat tone here. Draw them in beam on ink, then verify them desaturated.

The accent, and the one thing it must not reach. The watch accent colour is beam #FCCC0C (both AccentColor.colorset files are empty stubs today, so Story 21.3 adds a definition rather than changing one). Apple applies the accent to the app's title string in the status bar — which on the watchOS barcode screen is the card's name. The barcode screen therefore overrides the tint to cream, as it already overrides dark mode. The fallback tint #1A73E8 at WatchComplicationWidget.swift:75 is the pre-Cardì blue and goes with the rest of the palette.
